PT 2Vocabulary You Must Own▼
learn first
IndemnityRestoring the insured to the same financial position they were in before the loss. She calls this "the definition of insurance." If you know one word, know this one. An insurance contract is a contract of indemnity: insureds may recover no more than the amount of their loss.
RiskThe potential/chance of loss. Not "the cause of a loss" (that's already happened, so it isn't risk anymore).
Pure riskChance of loss or no loss. Insurable.
Speculative riskChance of loss or gain (gambling, market investing). Not insurable.
LossAn unintentional decrease in the value of an asset due to a peril; the insurable event; the amount the insurer must pay. Not "the probability an event will occur."
PerilThe cause of the loss (the accident, the fire, the death).
HazardA condition that increases the chance of loss. ("Hazard ahead" on Waze.)
Moral hazardIncreased chance of loss due to the insured's dishonest tendencies.
Loss exposureA situation that has the potential for loss.
Law of large numbersThe more times an event is repeated, the more predictable the outcome becomes. The statistical basis of insurance.
RepresentationA statement the applicant makes to the insurer on the application (age, health, habits). May be altered or withdrawn only before the policy is in effect.
Materiality / material factA fact so important it could affect the premium or the insurer's decision to issue. Must be disclosed.
ConcealmentDeliberately withholding a material fact. On intentional concealment, the injured party (the insurer) is entitled to rescind the contract.
ReinsuranceAn insurer transferring loss exposure from policies it wrote to another insurer, to free up capital and write more business.
Captive insurerAn insurer created by a parent company for the sole purpose of insuring the parent company's loss exposures.
Life settlement / viatical settlementSelling your existing life policy to a third party for less than face value while alive; the buyer becomes beneficiary and collects the full death benefit at your death. A life settlement broker represents the individual wanting to sell their policy.
Adhesion (contract of)The contract is written by one party (the insurer) and the other party may only accept or reject it — no negotiation.
Unilateral (contract)Only one party (the insurer) makes a legally enforceable promise. You can stop paying premiums with no legal consequence; the insurer must pay a valid claim.
AleatoryUnequal exchange of value (you pay $25, they may owe $500,000).
UnderwritingThe process by which the insurer decides whether to issue the policy.
SolventThe insurer has the assets to pay its claims. A required "competent party" condition for the insurer side of the contract.
ConservationAn agent's attempt to stop the replacement of an existing policy.
Twisting / unnecessary replacementReplacing a policy when the new one holds no greater financial benefit to the client. Prohibited.
PT 3Contract Law▼
heavily tested

The 4 requirements of a legal contract (memorize cold)

1. Legal purposeYou can't contract to do something illegal
2. Competent partiesBoth of sound mind; the insurer must be solvent
3. ConsiderationSomething of value exchanged. It does NOT have to be equal. (Classic trick answer: "equal consideration is required" = FALSE.)
4. Offer and acceptanceThe application is the offer; the insurer's issued policy/counter-offer and your first premium payment complete it

Because insurance meets all four, a policy is legally a written contract.

The entire contract

= the policy + the attached application. (Why: within the 2-year contestable period the insurer must be able to reference exactly what you represented.)

Parties to an insurance contract (4)

Insurer (company) · Insured (person/thing covered) · Owner (pays, controls, names beneficiary) · Beneficiary (receives proceeds).

TRICKOn "which of these is NOT protected by the guaranty association," the odd one out is the insurer — the other three are all on the client side.

Insurable interest

Must exist at the time of application. You have it in yourself, spouse, children, parents, business partners, key employees. You do NOT have it in a business owner → ordinary customer relationship.

Other contract facts

  • Backdating = making the policy effective on an earlier date than the present (used to lock in a lower age).
  • Conditional receipt vs. binding receipt: a binding receipt always provides coverage starting from the date of the receipt ("bound = held to it"). A conditional receipt only provides coverage if conditions are met.
  • Good health statement — required at policy delivery if no initial premium was collected with the application.
  • Application changes must be initialed by the applicant.
PT 4Insurers, Regulation & Marketing▼
GK + CA flags

Insurer classification by location

DomesticOrganized in your own state
ForeignOrganized under the laws of another U.S. state
AlienOrganized under the laws of another country
Admitted / authorizedApproved to transact business in this state. Non-admitted = not approved.

Insurer classification by ownership

Mutual insurerOwned by policyholders; issues participating policies that pay dividends from divisible surplus
Stock insurerOwned by shareholders; typically non-participating (no dividends to policyholders)

Divisible surplus (how dividends work)

Income − liabilities = surplus. The mutual insurer divides the surplus among participating policyholders as dividends, paid annually. Dividends are not guaranteed and are not taxable (treated as a return of premium, since premiums are paid with after-tax dollars).

Dividend options (valid): cash · reduce premium · paid-up additions · accumulate at interest · one-year term. NOT a dividend option: "monthly income payments." The dividend option is selected by the owner at the time of policy purchase.

Regulation

  • Insurance is regulated by the states, not federally — claim settlement practices are regulated by state insurance departments. (This is why you must study your own state chapter.)
  • Primary objectives of regulation: protect policyowners, ensure solvency, enforce insurance code. NOT interpreting policy provisions — the insurer's own contract language governs that.
  • Prior approval jurisdiction — rates must be approved by the insurance department before use.
  • States have no jurisdiction over Medicare (federal program).
  • Rating service companies (A.M. Best, Moody's, S&P) exist to determine the financial strength of an insurer. Citing a rating is NOT advertising. Advertising = illustrations, sales presentations, direct mail from the agency.
  • Advertising that your insurer is a member of the guaranty association = an unfair method of competition. (Prohibited everywhere.)
  • Agent record retention: 5 years on transaction records. [VERIFY-TN]
  • Federal offenses by an agent: embezzlement, fraud, falsifying records. Misrepresentation on an application is NOT a federal offense.
  • Misappropriating fiduciary funds = theft.
  • Life agent = an individual who transacts life, disability, or life & accident/health insurance on behalf of an insurer.
  • If an agent sells a policy for an insurer they aren't appointed with, a notice of appointment must be submitted to the commissioner.
  • NOT an act of solicitation: publishing a magazine where one of the advertisers happens to be an insurer.
  • Not an unfair claims settlement practice: advising a claimant to hire an attorney.

Federal acts to know

McCarran-Ferguson Act (1945)Leaves insurance regulation to the states. (The concept is what matters for the general portion.)
Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)Protects consumers with guidelines regarding credit reporting and distribution
ERISA (1974)Governs disclosure and reporting in group plans
Non-discrimination rulesRetirement plans may not favor highly compensated employees. Ethnicity is never collected on a life application.
🌴 CA-ONLY — do not memorize for TN; get the TN equivalents instead • Unwillful unfair method of competition: $5,000 max penalty per violation.
• McCarran-Ferguson violation (obtaining personal client info without a legitimate reason): $10,000 minimum.
• Insurance solicitor — a CA license class; a person employed to help an agent or broker sell insurance.
• Life and disability analyst — CA license required to charge a fee to review an existing policy.
• CA free look: 10–30 days; 30 days if the owner is age 60+. [VERIFY-TN — TN's free look length is different; confirm in your XCEL TN chapter.]
• CA requires disclosure of expunged misdemeanor convictions on a license application.
• CA required policy contents: policy period, premium, parties, what/who is insured. NOT the insurer's financial rating.
• CA solvency: insurer must hold assets covering liabilities + reinsurance of outstanding risks + additional assets equal to paid-in capital.
• CA Life & Health Guarantee Association does not cover self-funded plans.
PT 5Types of Life Insurance▼
know cold

Term

Temporary, pure death protection, no cash value, coverage ends when the term expires.

Level termConstant face amount
Decreasing termFace amount declines over time. The mortgage-protection answer.
Increasing termFace amount rises
Renewable termRenewable without evidence of insurability, but at a higher premium at each renewal (you're older)
Convertible termConvert to permanent without proving insurability

Whole Life (keyword: LEVEL)

Permanent. Level premium, level death benefit, guaranteed cash value, endows at 100/121. Level premium permanent insurance accumulates a reserve that will eventually equal the face amount of the policy.

  • Limited pay (e.g., 20-pay life) — paid up early; a "paid-up dividend option" lets dividends pay the policy up early.
  • Single premium — one lump-sum payment.

Universal Life (keyword: FLEXIBLE)

Permanent with flexible premiums and an adjustable death benefit; combines a savings element with a flexible premium option. Interest-sensitive. Partial surrender — the provision allowing the owner to withdraw cash value.

Variable Life (keyword: INVESTMENT CHOICE / SEPARATE ACCOUNT)

Cash value held in the insurer's separate account and invested at the owner's direction. It is a security — requires a securities registration in addition to a life license.

Variable Universal Life

Flexible premiums + flexible death benefit + owner picks the investments. The exclusive VUL feature is the right to select the investment expected to provide the greatest return.

Endowment

Pays the face amount to the insured if they survive to the stated date/age.

Other classifications

Non-medical lifeNo physical exam / no labs, but you still must answer the medical questions on the application
Guaranteed issueNo medical underwriting at all
Second-to-die (survivorship)Pays on the death of the second insured. Best answer for the couple with a financially dependent handicapped child where one death wouldn't be disastrous but both would.
Family policyChild riders cover children born after the issue date too; converting a child's coverage to permanent does not require evidence of insurability
PT 6Provisions, Riders & Options▼
#1 trap zone

Key provisions

Grace periodProtects against unintentional lapse; premium may be paid late without loss of coverage
ReinstatementRestores a policy that has already lapsed
IncontestabilityAfter 2 years, the insurer can no longer contest for misstatement/concealment
Suicide clauseNo death benefit for suicide in the first 2 years (premiums returned); protects against adverse selection
Misstatement of age/sexNo penalty — the death benefit is prorated to what the premium would have bought at the correct age. (If Jerry paid $12/1,000 instead of $15/1,000, the benefit = 12/15 of the face amount.)
Automatic premium loanPays a missed premium via a loan against cash value
Policy loanLoans reduce the net death benefit if unrepaid (borrow $15k against $25k CV on a $100k policy → beneficiary gets $85k)
Collateral assignmentNormally associated with bank loans
Free lookCancel for a full refund within the state-set window [VERIFY-TN]
Limit of liabilityThe policy's face amount

Common riders

  • Waiver of premium — takes effect when the insured becomes totally disabled.
  • Accidental Death Benefit (ADB) — doubles the face amount if death is accidental. Does NOT affect cash value (face amount and cash value are separate).
  • Guaranteed insurability option — buy additional coverage with no evidence of insurability.
  • Accelerated Death Benefit (ABR / living benefits) — pays part of the death benefit early for terminal, critical, or chronic illness / critical injury. This is the alternative to a life/viatical settlement.
  • Spousal / child riders, common carrier accidental death.
  • A "rider" = an endorsement that modifies the provisions of the policy.
❌ WATCH OUT"Extended term" is NOT a rider and NOT a settlement option — it's a nonforfeiture option.

The three option families (don't mix these up — the #1 trap in the series)

Nonforfeiture options
Question stemWhat happens to cash value if you stop paying / surrender
ChoicesCash surrender · Extended term · Reduced paid-up
Dividend options
Question stemWhat to do with your share of divisible surplus
ChoicesCash · Reduce premium · Paid-up additions · Accumulate at interest · One-year term
Settlement options
Question stemHow the beneficiary receives the death benefit
ChoicesLump sum · Interest only · Fixed period · Fixed amount · Life income
  • Interest option = beneficiary leaves the principal to accumulate and takes only the interest.
  • Life income = stated amount for life, no residual value to a beneficiary.
  • A policy may be surrendered for cash value under the nonforfeiture provision.
  • If you elect extended term and the term expires — protection simply ends.

Beneficiaries

  • Revocable — owner may change freely.
  • Irrevocable — owner needs the beneficiary's signature/consent to change.
  • If the estate is named beneficiary, creditors can reach the proceeds. Naming a person (spouse, child, parent) keeps creditors out.
  • "Life insurance creates an immediate estate" = when the insured dies, a death benefit is paid.
PT 7Underwriting▼
GK section

Sources of underwriting information

✅ The application · Medical Information Bureau (MIB) reports · Consumer/investigative reports (credit, lifestyle) · Attending Physician Statement (APS) · medical exam/labs. ❌ NOT a source: the disclosure authorization response.

MIB

  • Members are life and health insurance companies — most U.S. life insurers belong.
  • Members must report medical ailments discovered during underwriting.
  • Practical takeaway: tell clients to disclose everything; the MIB will surface it anyway.
  • An APS request must be accompanied by a copy of the signed authorization.
  • HIV testing requires a signed consent form first.

Risk classification (premium from lowest to highest)

Preferred → Standard → Substandard (rated). Substandard charges the highest premium.

Premium components & modes

  • Premium rate components: mortality (affected by age and gender), interest, and expenses (the agent's commission is an insurer expense).
  • Mortality rate = number of deaths in a year vs. the number of people in a select group. Mortality tables = probability of death listed by year.
  • ❌ Dividends are NOT a component of premium determination.
  • Payment mode, cheapest → most expensive: annual → semiannual → quarterly → monthly. Monthly is the most expensive over a year (the "Costco bulk discount" logic).
  • Initial premium lowest → highest: "MOST" = Modified → Ordinary life → Single premium.

Elements of an insurable risk

Loss must be: due to chance, definite and measurable, statistically predictable, not catastrophic to the insurer, and part of a large homogeneous group. ❌ Trick answer: "the loss must be catastrophic" — FALSE, that's a disqualifier. For loss retention to work as a risk-management technique, the probability of loss must be KNOWN ("probability of loss is unknown" is the wrong-answer option).

Exclusions

  • Act of war — a policy exclusion, especially under group life (military members carry SGLI for this reason).
  • Suicide — excluded first 2 years.
  • Hazardous occupations/avocations — may be rated or declined.
  • ✅ Commercial aviation is normally COVERED (deemed safe overall).
  • Results clause — the military service exclusion that pays when death results from causes not connected to military service (e.g., the active-duty serviceman killed in an accident while home on leave).
PT 8Group Life▼
master + certs
  • The employer owns the master policy; each participant/employee receives a certificate of insurance. (Her "octopus" image: one master policy, many legs.)
  • No individual medical questions — you just opt in. Individual life requires medical questions; that's the core underwriting difference.
  • Contributory plan — part of the premium is paid by the employee. (Noncontributory = employer pays 100%.)
  • Conversion privilege — on termination, convert group term → individual permanent within 31 days, and NO proof of insurability is required. That "no proof of insurability" piece is the privilege. The individual (not the employer) pays the premium on the converted policy.
  • Employer-provided group life above $50,000 — the cost of the excess coverage is taxable income to the employee.
  • Act of war is excluded.
PT 9Annuities▼
GK section
  • Definition: the systematic liquidation of a sum of money. You cannot outlive the income (guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit).
  • Two phases: Accumulation (paying in / growing) → Annuitization (the period when the accumulated value is paid out).
  • Immediate vs. Deferred — the difference is the time at which benefit payments start. Immediate = within 12 months of purchase. Deferred = a minimum of 12 months after purchase.
  • Life annuity certain — guarantees a stated number of income payments whether or not the annuitant is alive to receive them.
  • Variable annuity — backed by the insurer's separate account.
  • Equity indexed annuity — offers a minimum guaranteed rate, downside protection in a declining market, and long-term inflation protection. ❌ It does NOT offer a maximum interest rate that increases annually.

PT 11Taxation & Qualified Plans▼
concepts only

Death benefits

Exempt from federal income tax. ⚠ NOT exempt from estate tax. If a wealthy client's estate exceeds the federal exemption, the death benefit can push them over — a real planning issue, and a distinction the exam likes.

Cash value & living access

  • Cost recovery rule on surrender: the cost basis (what you paid in) is exempt from taxation; gain above basis is taxable.
  • Policy loans are generally not taxable but reduce the net death benefit if unrepaid.

MEC (Modified Endowment Contract)

  • A policy issued after 1988 that fails the 7-pay test — i.e., it accumulates cash value faster/higher than the IRS allows.
  • Why it matters: it's treated as an investment vehicle, so pre-death distributions get different (worse) tax treatment: LIFO taxation plus a 10% penalty tax on premature withdrawals/loans before age 59½ (same as a 401(k)).
  • Risk product: IUL / cash-value-heavy policies are what turn into MECs if overfunded.

IRAs

Traditional IRA
ContributionsDeductible
DistributionsTaxed as ordinary income
InheritedBeneficiary pays income tax on withdrawals
Roth IRA
ContributionsNon-deductible
DistributionsTax-free
InheritedNo income tax

Qualified plans

  • Must be permanent, not temporary — a temporary plan is disqualified from favorable tax treatment.
  • Cannot favor highly compensated employees.
  • ❌ Contributions are NOT unlimited — federal contribution limits apply.
  • ESOP = Employee Stock Ownership Plan, typically invested in employer stock.
⚠ WATCH OUTDo not memorize her dollar figures. She quotes a $6,000 IRA limit and a $12.9M estate exemption from 2023 — both have changed. The exam tests concepts, not current-year IRS numbers.

PT 15Her Exam-Day Anxiety Trick▼
the Part 8 payoff
1Know your math before you walk in. TN Life: 68 scored questions, pass = scaled 70. Work out roughly how many you need right and write that number down.
2On the scratch paper they give you, draw a big upside-down "T" — creating two columns on top and a strip along the bottom.
3First pass: answer every question straight through. Use the computer's flag/review button on anything you're unsure of — and also write that question number along the bottom strip. Now you can see both which questions gave you trouble and how many.
4Stop. Breathe. Reset. Take a deliberate pause after your first pass.
5Second pass: go through the whole test again. For each question, tick either the "SURE" column or the "NOT SURE" column.
6When your SURE column hits your pass number (plus a small buffer), you're done. You can submit with confidence instead of grinding out the clock in a panic.
🧠 HER POINTMost exam anxiety comes from not knowing how you're doing. This gives you a live scoreboard.

Her other repeated tips

  • Later questions often answer earlier ones. She shows this twice — questions #3/#4 (currently insured) and #182/#183 (accidental death benefit) are literally inverses of each other. Flag and come back.
  • Read the stem for the trigger word first, then look at the answers.
  • Use process of elimination on "which is NOT…" questions — find the one that's not like the others (e.g., insurer vs. owner/insured/beneficiary).

Accuracy notes on the source material

NOTE: The instructor is a practicing agent, not a textbook, and she is candid that she filmed most of these after midnight. A few things to be aware of:
  • She occasionally swaps the words "insurer" and "insured" mid-sentence. Read for intent.
  • Her dollar figures are dated (2022–2023): the $6,000 IRA cap and $12.9M estate exemption have both changed.
  • She answers "mortality costs" for "what is an expense factor" — for the exam, remember the three premium components as mortality, interest, and expenses, with agent commissions clearly falling under expenses.
  • Her McCarran-Ferguson treatment is a California fine question. The general-knowledge point is that the act left insurance regulation to the states.
  • She is a Life-only licensee and says so — a couple of the questions (e.g., ERISA) lean group health and may not appear on a Life-only exam.
