Why the phases overlap. After you sit the exam there's a stretch where fingerprints clear, the NIPR application processes, and your license gets issued — days where you're waiting, not working. That's when the sales call training starts, so by the time your license lands you're ready to dial instead of starting over. The $2,500 campaign itself doesn't start yet, though — no ads, no spend, during Phase 1 or Phase 2. The clock only starts the day you're Green Lit and cleared to call. That's deliberate: every dollar of ad spend lands on a week you're actually working the phones.

This is a target, not a promise. Full-time in a standard-order state, 14 days is very achievable. Part-time runs longer. A few states (Kansas, Arkansas) require paperwork before the course and can add a week at the front. Your manager will tell you which track you're on at kickoff.

READ THIS FIRSTGetting licensed is your job right now. Everything else here comes second. You can't write a policy without a license, no matter how well you know the script. If a day forces you to choose between your course and your script work — the course wins.

But you won't spend two weeks alone with a textbook either. You're on the daily Daybreak calls from Day 1, and your manager checks in every day. The moment your exam is done, you spend four days learning the sales call while the paperwork clears — so you're dialing the week your license lands.
DO THISYou have two script documents. Use them differently.

The Script is what you read on the phone — the actual words.
The Study Guide is how you learn it — structure, logic, drills, self-tests.

Work from the Study Guide during your script blocks. Keep the Script open beside it.
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSThe course sets up the call. Underwriting, policy types, beneficiaries, effective dates — all exam material, and all of it comes back in Days 8–12 when you learn what to actually say about it. You learn the product first, then how to sell it.

Which ones matter most, and when

🧠 WHILE YOU'RE LICENSING (DAYS 1–10)
  • Monday Bootcamp — never miss it, it's built for exactly where you are.
  • Thursday team meeting — never miss it, that's your manager and your team.
  • Friday Agency Meeting — never miss it. Direction and momentum for the whole agency.
  • Tuesday Sales Training — attend, take notes on things that don't apply yet.
  • Wednesday Objection Handling — listen only. You haven't learned the script yet and this goes deep. Absorb the tone, don't try to keep up, don't feel behind. It'll land much better after Day 8.
  • Saturday Script Training — optional right now. Your course comes first on weekends.
  • Tuesday evening Leadership — optional. Worth catching occasionally if you want to build your own team eventually.
🧠 ONCE YOU'RE LEARNING THE CALL (DAYS 8–12)
  • Saturday Script Training becomes one of the best hours of your week. You'll be in the middle of learning the stages and this is live practice with people who run them daily. Go.
  • Wednesday Objection Handling stops being background noise — by Day 10 you'll have covered Stage 8 and the "I want to think about it" ladder, and this call will suddenly make sense.
WATCH OUTShow up even when you have nothing to report. Especially then. Agents who start skipping calls in week one are usually gone by week four. It's an hour a day — protect it.

DAY 8The Map + OPEN (Stages 1–3)▼
script · the map
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSBefore you memorize a single word, memorize the ten stages in order. If you know the map, you can survive any call. Agents who learn the opening perfectly and nothing else get lost the second a client does something unexpected.
  • ☐Learn the color system — blue = say it · black = stop talking · red = never read aloud · yellow = study noteAGENT
  • ☐Learn the three rules — slow and low tone · never read red · write everything down, every presentationAGENT
  • ☐Learn the four phases — OPEN · LEARN · SHOW · CLOSE — and the one job of eachStudy Guide, Step One. Four is easier to hold than ten, and it's what gets you unstuck on a live call.AGENT
  • ☐Write the ten stages out by hand, in orderAGENT
  • ☐Say all ten from memory, out loud, without lookingAGENT
  • ☐Know where the call ends — Stage 10 is the finish line, referrals come much laterAGENT
  • ☑Day 1 bar: recite the four phases with their jobs, then all ten stages cold, in order, without the page. That's the whole test today — no lines yet.AGENT

Then Phase OPEN — Stages 1, 2, 3

  • ☐Stage 1 — The Open. Confirm the name, reference their requestIncludes the branch: have they already been contacted or not. Both paths land in the same place.AGENT
  • ☐Stage 2 — Positioning. Manager assigned to their request · 4–5 quick questions · "the biggest thing I can do is make sure you actually qualify"That last line flips the call — you're not selling, you're screening.AGENT
  • ☐Stage 3 — Credentials text. License and business card sent earlyAGENT
  • ☐Objection: "Who do you work for?" Independent broker → name A-rated carriers → pivot to pen and paperAGENT
  • ☐Memorize the Phase 1 anchor lines word-for-wordStudy Guide — three lines from Stages 1 and 2.AGENT
  • ☐Cumulative run: Stages 1–3, out loud, with a partnerAGENT
WATCH OUTStage 1 works when you sound slightly unsure and friendly — like a person, not a call center. Most new agents deliver it too confidently and sound like a telemarketer. Try it too casual a few times on purpose so you can hear the difference.

The "who do you work for" answer ends on a question. That's deliberate — it hands control back to you. Don't stop after "we're private brokers" and wait for approval.
DAY 9LEARN (Stages 4–5)▼
biggest day
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSYour biggest day, and the best exam overlap in the whole week. Questions 1–5 are pure underwriting — the same material as the risk-classification chapter of your course. If you can explain why a carrier asks about A1C, you're learning both at once.
  • ☐Q1 — Date of birthAGENT
  • ☐Q2 — MedicationsIncluding the insulin-or-pills follow-up, the A1C question, and past prescriptions.AGENT
  • ☐Q3 — Heart attack, stroke, cancer, kidney or liver disease · major surgeriesAGENT
  • ☐Q4 — SmokingAGENT
  • ☐Q5 — Height and weightNote the different wording for male vs. female on the weight question.AGENT
  • ☐Q6 — Their main goal. The most important question in the scriptTheir answer decides which products you present and whether Stage 5 happens at all. Drill this one hardest.AGENT
  • ☐Q7 — Existing coveragePrivate / employer / VA, and the follow-ups under each.AGENT
  • ☐Paper discipline — every answer written down, every presentationStage 9 is built on these notes. Re-asking sounds like you weren't listening.AGENT
  • ☐Cumulative run: Stages 1–5, out loudAGENT
DO THISHave your partner answer like a real client — vague, chatty, jumping around. The questions are easy. Holding the thread while someone rambles is the skill you're actually building.
  • ☐Stage 5 — Term vs. Permanent. Only for under-65 clients leaving money behindLearn when NOT to run it — using it on the wrong client wastes the call.AGENT
DAY 10SHOW (Stages 6–8)▼
heaviest day
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSHeaviest product-knowledge day, and it lines up with the policy-types chapter of your course. If you're fuzzy on how permanent coverage builds cash value, you'll feel it today — that's an exam gap as much as a script gap.
  • ☐Stage 6 — Pen and paper. No prices until they're holding a penAGENT
  • ☐Stage 7a — Living benefits. Read the explanation whether they say they know it or notAGENT
  • ☐Stage 7b — Gold. $30,000 FE / $100,000 IUL-Term · $200–250/moAGENT
  • ☐Stage 7c — Silver. $20,000 FE / $75,000 IUL-Term · $125–199/moAGENT
  • ☐Stage 7d — Bronze. $15,000 FE / $50,000 IUL-Term · $60–124/moAGENT
  • ☐Rebuild the medal study card from memory — no notesAGENT
  • ☐Memorize the Gold framing line word-for-word"While this isn't the option I typically recommend…" — it's what makes Silver their idea.AGENT
  • ☐Cumulative run: Stages 1–8, out loudAGENT
WATCH OUTMemorize the price bands cold. Fumbling a number during the presentation costs more credibility than any other stumble in the call.
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSThe Gold framing — "this isn't the option I typically recommend, but I'm required to show you all your options" — is what makes Silver feel like the client's own idea. Understand the mechanism, don't just memorize the sentence.

Then Stage 8 — the close and its objection

🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSStage 8 is short, but it's the hinge of the whole call — and the objection attached to it kills more sales than anything else in this business. That's why it gets its own day.
  • ☐Stage 8 — the small yes. "Which option would you want to try to qualify for?"You never ask them to buy. This is the most important idea in the entire script.AGENT
  • ☐The can't-decide branch. "Are you open to any suggestions?" → Silver or Bronze → "we can always add coverage later but we can't decrease it"AGENT
  • ☐The bridge. Know immediately · no medical exam · green light or explore other optionsAGENT
  • ☐"I want to think about it" — all five rungsAn escalation ladder, not a menu. Work down only as far as you need. Most calls end at rung one or two.AGENT
  • ☐Practice rung 4 with the silence — timed if you have toAGENT
  • ☐Memorize the four Stage 8 anchor lines word-for-wordThe small yes, the add-vs-decrease line, "no commitments today," and "just numbers on paper."AGENT
WATCH OUTRung 4 — "what were you hoping to hear from another insurance company?" — is followed by silence. You stay quiet and let them tell you what's actually wrong. Nearly every new agent fills that gap. It will feel much longer than it is.
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSThis objection almost always lands right after the quotes. In the client's head it's about price — but usually it means value didn't get built well enough earlier. The real fix is often back in Stage 4 or 7, not in the rebuttal.

DAY 13Launch — Lead System + Green Light▼
launch day
Lead system in the morning, Green Light in the afternoon. Live leads next.

Morning — Set Up Your Lead System

🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSThis isn't a purchased pack you have to make last. The moment you're Green Lit this afternoon, your $50/day ad spend goes live — that's Day 1 of your funded 30 days. From here on, real people fill out a form and land in your Sheet every day, continuously, for the next month. How you respond decides how much of that $2,500 actually turns into production. The single biggest lever is speed: someone who filled out a form five minutes ago is far more receptive than the same person tomorrow.

Read the Lead Response System before your first call. This checklist covers the setup; that document covers the method.
  • ☐Read Working Your Leads — The Lead Response System start to finish before your first callAGENT
  • ☐Learn the calling-hours rules — 8am–9pm in the lead's time zone, tighter in FL, OK, CAThis is federal law, not a guideline. $500 per violation — and it applies regardless of how the lead reached you.AGENT
  • ☐Pick your schedule tier — full-time, part-time evenings, or part-time daytimeDecide with your manager and commit to it. A schedule you actually hit beats an ambitious one you abandon.MANAGER
  • ☐Set up your notification habit so you know the moment a new lead landsAGENT
  • ☐Learn the disposition codes coldSOLD · DNC · BAD NUMBER · HARD NO · PRESENTED/NO SALE · CALLBACK · NO CONTACTAGENT
  • ☐Set up your tracking — dials, dial hours, contacts, presentations, applications, time-to-first-callAGENT
  • ☐Know the attempt standard: 6–8 attempts across different days and windows before a no-contact is deadAGENT
WATCH OUTThe DNC rule has no exceptions. If someone asks off your list, write it down immediately and never call that number again — not next week, never. One "let me try one more time" is a violation.

Afternoon — Your Green Light

DO THISWhat the Green Light is: you run the whole call — Stages 1 to 10 — with your manager playing a real client. About half an hour. Pass and your Sheet is live for you the same day.

This is an in-house check, not a license or an official credential. Your license is the state exam you already passed. This is just us making sure you're ready before the campaign starts spending real money on leads for you.
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSWhat you're graded on. No surprises:
  • You never read a red note out loud
  • No prices before they have pen and paper
  • You offer the credentials text in Stage 3
  • All seven qualifying questions asked and written down — they'll check your paper
  • Stage 8 asked right: "try to qualify for," not "want to buy"
  • Stage 10 done in full, not skipped
  • You get to Stage 10 without stopping to start over
Plus four judgment calls: your tone, whether Stage 4 flows like a conversation, how you handle objections (including holding the silence), and whether you stay casual through the Social and banking asks.

You'll get two objections you're not expecting. One of them will be "I want to think about it."
  • ☐Full run-through, Stages 1–10, with a manager or agent lead playing a difficult clientMANAGER
  • ☐Handle at least two objections cold — unannouncedAGENT
  • ☐Clear all seven non-negotiables + four judgment callsMANAGER
  • ☐Walk your manager through your response plan — schedule tier, response speed, calling windowsAGENT
  • ☐Green Light — cleared for live leadsMANAGER
DO THISThis isn't a hurdle, it's protection. Your campaign is live and spending real money every day — you don't want to burn your first week's leads figuring out what you should have practiced.
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSReferrals stay closed until you're fluent through Stage 10. Learn the sale first. Referrals are a bonus, not part of the sale — and pushing for one on a rushed call costs the goodwill you just spent forty minutes building.
