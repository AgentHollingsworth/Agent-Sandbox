Working Your Leads
The Lead Response System

This is not a cold-calling method — every name in your Sheet already asked for this call.

Tap any section to open it.
IDEAThe Core Idea▼
read first
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSEvery lead in your Sheet clicked an ad and filled out a form asking about life insurance. Nobody is cold. Your job isn't to earn the right to be on the phone with a stranger — it's to follow up, fast and well, with someone who already raised their hand. That's a customer-support conversation wearing a sales script, not a cold-call.

Two different jobs live inside this document. Job one is speed: respond to brand-new leads immediately. Job two is persistence: keep working the leads who haven't answered yet, using a rotation across the day. Job one is the highest-leverage thing you'll do. Job two matters because a phone call is still a phone call no matter how warm the lead was.

JOB 1Speed-to-Lead▼
highest leverage
DO THISCall every new lead as close to the moment they land in your Sheet as you possibly can. If you're at your desk, that means within minutes, not hours. A lead who filled out a form ninety seconds ago is thinking about coverage right now.
🧠 WHY THIS MATTERSSpeed is the single biggest lever you have. With a form-fill lead, the person is expecting to hear from someone, and how fast that happens changes whether they remember filling out the form at all. Contact and close rates drop sharply once you're past the first several minutes.
  • ☐Check your Sheet (or notification) constantly during working hours — new leads don't wait for your next break
  • ☐Call first, text second if no answer — a short text within a few minutes still rides the moment
  • ☐If you can't answer immediately, call the newest lead back the second you're free — newest first, not oldest
  • ☐Track your own time-to-first-call number — it predicts your contact rate better than anything else here
JOB 2Working the Backlog▼
persistence

Not everyone answers the first call. A form-fill doesn't mean someone's sitting by the phone — it means they're interested. The fix is a rotation across the day, because people are reachable at different times: someone who doesn't pick up at 10am might pick up at 6pm without fail.

Sort your Sheet every session into two groups

NEW
What it isLanded since your last session, not yet called
What you doCall these first, in the order they arrived. See Job One.
BACKLOG
What it isAlready called once, no contact yet, or due for another attempt
What you doWork these across your calling windows, oldest-due first

LAWThe Rules Before Anything Else▼
federal law
WATCH OUTCalling hours are federal law, not a guideline, and none of that changes because the lead is inbound. You may only call between 8:00 AM and 9:00 PM in the lead's local time zone — not yours. Fines start at $500 per call and go to $1,500 if willful.

Our working window is 9:00 AM – 8:00 PM lead-local. It keeps you legal in every state without having to think about it.

DEAD?How Many Attempts Before a Lead Is Dead?▼
6–8 attempts
DO THISSix to eight attempts, across different days and windows, before you call a no-contact dead. This doesn't change because the lead came in warm.
DO THISLeave a voicemail on the first attempt, then roughly every third one. Keep it short: your name, that you're following up on the coverage information they requested, and your callback number said slowly, twice.

Text is underused — use it

A short text after an unanswered call gets responses that calls alone don't — especially in the first hour after a fresh lead lands and doesn't pick up. Same rules apply: texts count as solicitation under the same 8am–9pm restriction, and a text to someone on your DNC list is the same violation as a call.
